# Educational and geographic sources

Compiled and checked 27 October 2026. Summaries, quiz wording, translations and illustrations were written for this application. External sources support the facts; their prose and photographs have not been copied into the guides. Links open only after user action.

## Interpretation

- Coordinates use **decimal degrees, WGS84**, north/east positive. Display precision is two decimal places; stored precision is typically four. They are approximate educational points, not surveyed positions, political borders or navigation instructions.
- Country pins are representative inland locations. Forest/reef pins represent a point within a large system. A country marker is not necessarily its capital or geographic centroid.
- The seven-continent model uses **Australia** as the continent and **Oceania** as the larger geographic region. See [National Geographic's map policy](https://www.nationalgeographic.org/national-geographic-map-policy). Continental classifications can differ between educational traditions.
- The globe texture is a December 2004 satellite composite. It does not show current conditions. Atmospheric glow, stars and lighting are an artistic educational visualization.
- No borders are drawn or claims of sovereignty made. The South Pole pin's longitude is conventionally 0°, since all meridians meet at the pole. The camera stops at 85° S to avoid an orbit singularity; the pin remains at the true 90° S.
